Industry-Tailored First Aid Workshop

A practical, scenario-based approach to HLTAID009 Provide cardiopulmonary resuscitation and HLTAID011 Provide First Aid, designed to move learners quickly from theory into realistic emergency response.

The challenge

Making compliance feel like real preparation.

First Aid training can easily become slide-heavy and passive. I wanted the practical component to feel closer to the conditions learners may actually face: limited time, incomplete information, other people needing direction, equipment to locate and a casualty whose condition may change.

The workshop was built around repeated practical rehearsal rather than long blocks of presentation. Learners worked through realistic maritime and onboard scenarios, made decisions, communicated with each other and then completed the related reporting and handover activities.

Design principle

Give learners enough structure to stay safe and competent, but enough uncertainty that they still have to think.

Workshop design

Built around activity, not waiting.

The session structure was designed so learners were continually doing something useful: responding, assisting, observing, communicating, documenting or receiving feedback.

01

Rotating team roles

Learners rotate between primary responder, assistant responder and observer / emergency-services / recorder roles so participation stays active.

02

Practical stations

CPR and First Aid are broken into focused stations with equipment, scenario prompts, assessor observation and repeated skill rehearsal.

03

Unknown scenarios

Learners can be given emergency scenarios without prior knowledge, requiring them to assess the situation rather than simply reproduce a script.

04

Complication cards

Changing conditions such as worsening symptoms, rescuer fatigue, interference, noise or delayed emergency response add realistic pressure.

05

Communication & handover

Learners practise delegating tasks, communicating with other responders, escalating care and providing a clear verbal handover.

06

Incident reporting

Scenario work flows into documentation, with additional details supplied progressively to simulate workplace information gathering.

Assessment approach

Practice can be coached. Competency still has to be demonstrated.

During formative practice, learners can receive prompting, repetition and feedback. Final competency decisions are based on the learner's own observed performance and the required evidence for the relevant unit.

The workshop design also supports mixed experience levels. More experienced learners can take on additional complications or team-lead responsibilities, while less experienced learners can build confidence through guided practice and repetition before final assessment.
